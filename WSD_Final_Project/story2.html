Black Sand

Black Sand The sky was gray on the horizon. For as long as she remembered, it had been painted gray, the color of emptied rainclouds perpetually seeping into the heavens, like soaked ink brushes resting on white paper, their loads swirling in a small glass cask of water. The cold sea breeze gently blew in, carrying with it the scent of salt and dead fish, a certain warmth emanating upwards from the pool she was sitting high above of. The rocks beneath her were sharp and jagged, biting into her skin, threatening to tear into it and bleed her delicate fingers, adding the tiniest volume to the vast black ocean far below her. The waves lapped against the stone softly, with futility, fully knowing that it would never be able to topple down such a great structure of sand colored rock, bleached by the sun that had existed once before centuries ago; still the ocean tried and, painting the rock briefly white with the foam of its creases, failed miserably in its endeavor as the sound of lapping water echoed, reverberated by the rocky and uneven surface of the cliff.
Beating in a strange rhythm different from that of the waves, Auphel gently slapped her tail against the rock beneath her, salted dry long hair heavy as the seabreeze tried to lift it up from her wet shoulders. A chill took over her body and Auphel shivered. Any onlooker would have thought the young woman to be cold and offered to wrap a coat or quilt around her, but it was not cold that compelled Auphel to burst into violent motions, but something much more fierce. She did not wrap her atoms around her shoulders, but instead clenched her fists, dragging her waterlogged fingers across the jagged stone. The warm sensation of blood made her sigh with relief and satisfaction of a perverse nature.
“Auphel!” a shrill and crooked voice called out, making her flinch and her shoulders shrink. “Are you still there? The meat’s done drying girl! Get your worthless bones down here already!”

Auphel brought her hands up and stared at the bleeding palms, gritting her teeth all the while. She slapped her tail against the rock, much more loudly and strongly than her idle beating, feeling faint trails of blood there as well.

“Bah, you useless wretch!” The voice shouted, and in a much lower and graver tone, the figure that spoke spat a strange muttering, the bizarre song of a swollen and twisted tongue, a horrible sound that made Auphel’s skin quite literally crawl along her sinew and bones. Paralyzed by pain, she watched as her lower half—previously sown together by blackened scales—was now split down the middle, the single tail separating into two limbs: long slender legs, covered in black fragments that slowly dissolved against the gentle breeze, revealing the pale cold skin beneath. Auphel immediately brought up her knees to her chest and cradled her shins and ankles, shivering, overwhelmed by emotion. The reunion lasted a moment before she finally stood up and returned to the prison she called home, walking down the path that had at first led her to that secret opening on the face of the cliff, following the scent of burning smoke, cooking meat and evaporating blood.

At the foot of the cliff, established on a beach very near the sea, was a small settlement made up of very little: a wooden shack high on the ground, a fire ring made up of giant rocks sitting on the edge of the sand, fishing poles of various sizes and a large crude knife; inside the stone ring, a fire was burning, and placed over it was the corpse of a giant shark, its skin in the process of cooking, on the verge of charring. Auphel looked around, but beyond the items that made up her “home” there was no one in sight. She looked towards the water: hanging from a large log, a long black bag of cloth was half submerged in the water. Approaching it with curiosity, the sound of a door slamming shut made her ears twitch in reaction; the shrill voice spoke again, this time with less emotion and much less urgency.

“It’s all dried up,” the voice said, stepping down from the solid grass into the unstable sand, “you’re going to need to fetch a new one. Soon.”
Auphel said nothing, watching the twisted hag walk down to the fire. A hunched figure, draped in black salt encrusted cloth, long strands of white hair hanging from the pulled cloak, her hands wrinkled by age, clutching onto a long wooden stick with which she prodded the ground. Approaching the ring, the hag reached out towards the cooking carcass and ripped a piece out from the shark’s belly, the hag’s skin and the ripped flesh smoking, biting into it with greed and a ravenous hunger; smoke ebbed from her mouth everytime the ugly cavity opened ajar to chew its contents.

“Come and eat, girl,” the hag said, sitting down on a rock set off to the side, “but not before getting that thing off there. Don’t want the rot ruining my meal.” Auphel walked towards the stone ring and picked up the crude piece of metal by the handle, carving out a piece of meat for herself and placing it on top of a rock, its smoked scent ebbing up towards her nostrils; her lips twisted with disgust. Moving towards the log arched over the sea, Auphel stepped into the black water—knee deep, the salt stinging her palms as she swam through it—reaching up towards the soggy piece of rope made taut by the weight it carried. She sliced it with ease and accidentally cut into the cloth at the same time, a sudden burst of thick moist air blasting her face with potency, the smell of waterlogged rot inundating her tear ducts and nostrils. Caught off guard, Auphel swatted her head away from it instinctively before turning back to look inside the bag: the remains of what had been once a man, now nothing more than a shriveled figure, drained of all life. A blue shade of gray, the half above his waist was dried up and hardened by salt, the skin stretched taut and biting into the bone like a tarp draped over metal structures; the lower half, however, was blue and swollen with water. Auphel looked at the remains with pity, closed her eyes and wished for his safe passing before letting the sack fall into the ocean, carried away beyond her sight into the distant darkness. The young woman watched the small island of black cloth ebb along the surface of the water, now almost indistinguishable from the rest, visible only when near the white foam tops of the waves that crashed against it. Watching the remains sink into the ocean, Auphel turned away and returned to the dying fire, taking the piece of flesh that she had placed atop a rock. It was cold and salty, littered with sand and moisture. On the other side of the fire, the hag had finished eating her fill, prompting her to pull the carcass down on top of the smoldering embers, extinguishing them in a most crude manner. With a quick glance, Auphel saw that half of the meat was missing, visibly torn apart by ugly human hands.

“Don’t eat too much,” the hag said, turning away from Auphel, heading towards the old shack, “lest you become unappealing to them.”
Auphel scoffed at the remark but said nothing, silently biting into the meat she had cut out for herself. It had become disgusting, but her face did not show it. She cut out another piece, half the size of the previous, and was about to bite into it as the hag turned from the entrance of the shack towards her and slammed her long stick into the dirt, conjuring up a single ugly sound from within her decayed throat. Immediately, the sand around Auphel began to gather at her feet, crawling up her legs, all the way to her waist—just below the belly button—at first as single grains, turning into thin strands, followed by bigger currents and even giant waves, wrapping themselves around her flesh. Layer after layer, each darker than the other, isolating one limb from the other, creating a long tail of shiny black scales with gray fins at the end, causing Auphel to lose her balance and fall on the sand along with her meal; one of its sides was now fully coated with it, lessening its appeal further. Groaning with frustration, she took the piece of cooked meat and dragged herself towards the shore, pulling what little weight she had with one free hand, certainly a bewildering sight to any onlookers. She knew, however, that no one would be looking her way: that prison which she called home could be barely considered an island, and ships seldom passed through there. After all, what business would they have in the middle of nowhere?

Close to the water, Auphel brought the meat towards the rising tide and washed the sand off the rapidly hardening meat, replacing the crystalized salt with a dissolved solution. She bit into it, gagged and spat out whatever was in her mouth, casting the piece of meat in another direction away from herself. Hot tears flowed down her cheeks, but she did not sob nor did she audibly cry. No, it was all in silence, her sorrow slowly washed away by the gentle breeze, an invisible hand brushing her cheeks with pity. She spat the salty phlegm in her mouth and dived into the ocean, navigating the black depths with ease, her body familiar with the surrounding waters, moving along an unseen path revealed only to her.
There, very far at the bottom along the foundations of the cliff, was a formation of rock where no creature lived. Like a clearing in between mountains, this was a clearing beneath the surface, a flat bed of rock between two ugly and malformed structures of oceanic rock the color of peach, its color washed away by the water and the amplified rays of the sun. Forming an unfinished arc, they provided some manner of shelter for the girl, hiding only half of her body from the elements and revealing the other half to the depths outside. This she was fine with, however, as it allowed her to be awakened by the warmth of the sun—even if it meant sleeping with her upper half exposed to the great emptiness. Exhausted by the weight of her solitude, Auphel lowered herself into the opening, certain that it had been made for her, as no other creature seemed to ever occupy it while she was gone. Maybe they knew she would kill whatever might try, and while those might have been silly musings at first, it was something that had slowly crawled its way up her back, becoming something that she was more than willing to do. Desperate, even. The water was cold around her bare shoulders, but with cold comes a subsequent warmth, and with that warmth comes the numbness, and with numbness comes release and the subsequent passing into sleep. She closed her eyes and let the waves lull her to sleep as the tide began to rise with greater viciousness. Its effects could be seen above, but there in that resting place, she could only hear the rushing currents from beyond to meet with the pillars of the earth, an exhilarating frontal collision powered by the thirst to test their strength, fully aware that there was no peril of death.

Though she hated the hag that imprisoned her and she hated her life above water, she felt a distant longing, a wistful sadness in her chest that made her long for something she couldn’t remember.
She thought about the blood on her palms, about the cloth sack and about the carcass, but no real realization came to mind, much to her chagrin. Still, for whatever reason, down below within the depths of the ocean, she felt a certain comfort that liberated her from her solitude. Feeling the presence of something greater reaching for her chest, enveloping her thin arms in a warm embrace, she dozed off, a soft smile parted on her lips.

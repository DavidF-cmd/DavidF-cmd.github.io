In the Tunnel


“In the end, you couldn’t save anyone.”

Those were the last words she said to me before she was taken by the train. The image of her soft smile, the red beret atop her short hazelnut hair, the little puff atop it like snow on a withering flower. The thin shawl wrapped around her shoulders, an old memory of the past; the long skirt painted by brown leaves, the white of noon on her chest. Standing on that platform, I could see the afterimage of her silhouette.
A final, fleeting moment is burned into my brain.

“Chin up, sport,” said the demon on my shoulder, “she was never really alive, that one. So don’t cry—save your tears! They are more precious than jewels these days.”

The words were clear and scathing, but they made no sense to me: I could not feel sorrow ebbing down my face. In fact, I couldn’t feel much of myself at all. It was a bizarre sensation, and though I continued to stare down that empty tunnel, there was nothing left but the breeze of tomorrow, and the buried tracks that lead down to the upside.

“Have you had enough?” the demon on my shoulder asked. “Or do you need to stare a while longer? Either way, we should get out of here soon, before the wind picks up further.”

I nodded, but I didn’t want to go. My body was like wet wood—heavy and weighed down by something immaterial—unable to look away from that spot where she had stood; my mind was caught, trapped in the last flash of memory, in that brief moment where that smile beamed at me. It was unlike anything I had seen before.

Warm. Resigned. Contemptuous. Disappointed. Exhausted.

Such was her face as she disappeared. Like a defeated addict, I once again ingested the contents of the past, and felt my soul fall into a numb state of hatred—for myself, for the world,for her decision to so easily cast me away. Brought down to the ground by the weight of my failings, I felt my body lifelessly touch the cold stone.

“Well, if you won’t do it, I’ll have to drag you myself.”

Overwhelmed by my own venomous creations, I had failed to realize the immobility I had subdued myself under, stupidly resting on my side, staring at the place where she had stood last, only coming to my senses as I was dragged away by the collar of my shirt. The sound of dirt dragging against my clothes filled my ears like the distorted calling of a bird, the grinding of an old machine, and all the while, as the tunnel became further and further away, I could see her standing there still.

That same smile, digging into my chest, a perpetual reminder of my own inadequacies, immortalized eternal.

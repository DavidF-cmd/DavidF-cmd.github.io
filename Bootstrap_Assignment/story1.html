Digital Twin

With eyes glazed over, the young woman sipped her coffee and stared at the screen before her, watching with mild interest as it filled up with lines of text, each a new garbled message. The device attached to the side of the monitor was working as intended, and with a sigh, Nutmeg watched the collective thoughts of a madman unfold. “A digital twin,” she said with a scoff, “what a preposterous idea.”

The gold, my only inmutable lead to her. It was ever present to my eyes and hers, a
beckoning light that made ache every bone, every fiber, every circuit in our bodies—even our souls ache in the presence of such a thing. Indeed, it is this very aching that leads her to cause havoc and leave chaos in her wake, fueled by a thirst for that gold.

I remember once, someone said to me that it was not gold, but an ailment; an illness of some sort. If so, then, why had that person decided to copy it onto her? A manufactured copy should be without flaws, and yet… I sought an answer, and there, perched upon a sturdy sign, its light now turned off by the owner of the establishment, looked opposite of my current target: a pharmaceutical joint of the shady kind. A rundown drug den to some, but that was a mere illusion, and the real deal hid beneath the floors. From up here, it looked as though it had already been dismantled completely; no doubt the law was already on its way there, bloodhounds on the scent of crime, eager to fetch their master’s prey. Little time left.
The streets reek with an old pestilence, deeply embedded from years of slaughter, both of flesh and spirit, the grasping hands of metal clinging on to failing temples of flesh, leaving naught but rotting scratches upon the land. I imagine the substances they give out here certainly help with easing the pain. Maybe that’s what our gold is. Door open, clean shots to the head. Nothing or no one worth wasting her dwindling force upon. A merciful end for those uninvolved, but I suspect the scenery twists into a more gruesome image the lower I descend. As I suspected, those found below fared a lot worse: upon a glance, one would assume it to be the work of a frenzied beast; truthfully, it wouldn’t be far off the mark. My digital twin, birthed from a scanner, a darkened image of myself, was vicious and unhinged, moreso when in search of something to ease her madness. Traces of gold here, but they are faint and disappearing. Empty vials strewn about the floor, in between pools of blood and depressions on the wood, easily mistaken as bullet casings by onlookers.
S.U.N Raw. Her favorite stimulant. A quick stop, but before what?

The sound of sirens echoes from afar, the tolling of the bell that rings for its followers to gather around it. Time’s up, and there’s still no way out of this hellscape: then, I see it. The gold that leads me to her brings me a window, half open, an exit into the dark. The patrolling hounds lingered there for some time, but none saw my escape. Surely, they would have torn me to shreds had they seen me escape such a viscerous scene, and in such a hurry too. The trail leads me further north, up end of the city, where Orpheus claimed his prize—and this time, did not squander it. It is a much more refreshing air that permeates this wing of the city—ill fit for a ravenous beast. Does Sigrid seek to disturb the peace here as well? The trails of gold seem to point in that direction, but rather than stretch through the streets on to the walled garden, where the concrete sprouts with the vigor of newborn trees, it seeks the shadows: the shrouded corners where they chose to hide, where the unsightly are stashed away. DNA, the code of life, the scripture written into our flesh. It is here, where the poet wrote of the conception of the earth and the heavens, where the hubris of man led them to toy with such a divine construct: biological machinations, engineering of life, cloning,vile creations and ever lasting horrors.

My digital twin. Why had she come to the place where she was conceived, not by flesh, but by machine? Was this just another game of hers? Or perhaps… There, I see her. She’s limping.
Hurt, maybe out of juice. She’s not responding, even as I call to her. Have her sensory functions already failed? I wrap my arms around her, but it is then that I understand. Though the gold leads me to her, this is but an illusion—a delusion, perhaps. A fake image, and a gun is put to my head. A mad cackling, and the click of metal. I had lost track of it. The trail of gold—I could see it now, leading backwards. Her voice was twisted, tired, failing. “You were always fooled by the gold, brother,” she said. There the last line ended, abruptly and with nothing else. The cursor blinked incessantly, and Nutmeg clicked her tongue in frustration. Slamming a button on her keyboard, she removed the device
from the monitor and stood up, stretching her arms and back with a sigh. Refreshed, she made her way towards the cylindrical tank set up behind her; the only source of illumination in that dark room, provided by the glowing liquid that filled up the construction. Pulling up a small ladder, she punched a combination onto the number pad and unlocked the top, digging out the body of a man that had been suspended inside the concoction with much struggle, cursing all the while. With the freshly made body resting on her lap, she dug into her pocket for a vial and attached a needle on one end, and the device on the other; and with no further delay, she injected it into the spinal cord, just below the nape. The device activated, the liquid drained and the body was given life once again, coughing up blue liquid and barely moving, speaking in a faint voice.
“Hildur…” it said, reaching up to her face.

“Wrong number, Mr. Sigurd,” Nutmeg said, gently moving the hand away from her face.

“Nutmeg,” the man said.

“I’ll ask you be more careful in the future,” Nutmeg said, standing up, “your new body will take a bit longer to make this time around.”

Sigurd, now more awake, sat up. “Noted,” he said. Then, “Any reason why?”

“Because you’re running through them like they’re clothes,” Nutmeg said, annoyed. “More importantly, did you find her?”

Sigurd nodded. “She’s falling apart. Went back to the walled garden. Lot of S.U.N Raw in her blood.”

Nutmeg draped a fur coat over the naked man. “She won’t last long then. What will you do when you find her?” she asked.

Sigurd slipped into the coat proper, but said nothing, and with the same silence, left the dark room behind him
